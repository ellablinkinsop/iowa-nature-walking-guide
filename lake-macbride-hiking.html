Exploring Lake Macbride State Park

Located just 15 minutes north of Iowa City near Solon, Lake Macbride State Park offers over 2,100 acres of scenic outdoor recreation. Featuring rugged shoreline footpaths, limestone multi-use trails, and unique coral reef spillway geology, Lake Macbride is Eastern Iowa's premiere destination for day hikes.

Beach to Spillway Trail

  • Length: 1.5 miles (one way)
  • Terrain: Crushed limestone and earth
  • Difficulty: Easy to Moderate

This trail connects the main swimming beach to the historic stone spillway. Following the natural curves of the shoreline, hikers enjoy shaded oak and hickory canopy cover with constant views of the water. The endpoint spillway is famous for exposed Devonian-era fossil formations that are visible when water levels allow.

Osprey Trail

  • Length: 2.3 miles loop
  • Terrain: Dirt paths with exposed roots and incline
  • Difficulty: Moderate

Winding through the quieter northern unit of the park, Osprey Trail takes hikers deep into mature hardwood forest and along elevated ridges overlooking hidden coves. Keep your eyes peeled for nesting raptors, white-tailed deer, and spring wildflowers. Waterproof footwear is recommended during spring thawing.

Lake Macbride Multi-Use Trail

  • Length: 5.0 miles
  • Terrain: Compact crushed limestone
  • Difficulty: Easy

Connecting Solon directly to Lake Macbride State Park, this wide, well-maintained limestone path is perfect for long-distance walkers, trail runners, and casual bicyclists. It offers gentle grades, clear sightlines, and multiple bench resting stops along restored tallgrass prairie stretches.
